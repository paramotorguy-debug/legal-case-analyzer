Network Pilot

Route every case to the firm most likely to win it.

An AI agent that learns your network's referral patterns and matches incoming cases to the right firm — with a visible rationale, decline-reason capture, and outcome tracking. Built for tenant law, personal injury, and the rest of the referral economy.

The Problem

Every firm in your network has a wheelhouse — case types, jurisdictions, signal patterns they want and don't want. Today, that knowledge lives in spreadsheets, intake notes, and someone's memory.

The cost: cases declined that should have been accepted, cases accepted that go nowhere, and referral fees walking out the door on every mismatch.

The Solution

A platform that watches every routing decision your network makes — accepts, declines, overrides, outcomes — and gets sharper at recommending the right firm for the next case.

No black box. Every suggestion comes with a rationale and a confidence level. The user always decides; the system always learns.

How it works

01  ·  INTAKE

Cases captured with structured signals

Case type drives the right intake questions — habitability cases ask about code violations and infestations; PI asks about hospitalization and corporate defendants. Universal fields cover documentation, timing, and vulnerability.

02  ·  MATCH

AI recommends a firm with rationale

Reads the case, every firm's profile, and the network's full referral history. Returns a single recommendation, the reasons behind it, and a confidence rating — never just a score.

03  ·  LEARN

Every decision becomes training data

Accepts, declines (with reasons), overrides, and final outcomes feed back. The system gets sharper on your network's actual patterns — not generic priors.

The math

Industry estimates put case intake at 90–120 minutes per matter, with another 20–40 minutes spent on routing — deciding which firm gets it, calling around, and re-routing after declines. For a network running 50 cases per month, that is 50–100 hours of pure routing labor before a single case is even worked.
